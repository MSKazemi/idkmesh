# X4 synthetic calibration: do pilot forecasts and their intervals hit a known truth?

**Refs:** issue #976 (X4, hypothesis H2), parent #969. **Evidence class: `synthetic`.**
Nothing on this page is a measurement of real coding agents. The empirical X4
result is [E046](https://github.com/MSKazemi/idkmesh/blob/main/experiments/E046-agent-value-forecast.md)
(point forecasts, one feasible held-out split, fragile). This page adds what E046
did not have: a check of the forecasters **and of the 95% intervals they report**
against a ground truth that is known exactly, as a function of pilot size k.

## Why a simulation, and what it can and cannot show

Real interval coverage needs the population's true coverage C(M) on held-out
data. Every SWE-bench split reachable today is already opened (E046 used them),
and the preregistered fresh data (E048,
[`PREREG_AGENT_VALUE_FORECAST_V2`](PREREG_AGENT_VALUE_FORECAST_V2.md)) does not
exist yet. Intervals computed on opened splits would be post-hoc. So this study
uses seeded simulators in which C(M) and the blind-spot floor 1 - C(M) are exact.

It can show whether the *machinery* (forecasters plus bootstrap intervals,
imported unchanged from the frozen E046 code and
[`forecast_intervals.py`](https://github.com/MSKazemi/idkmesh/blob/main/experiments/forecast_intervals.py))
is accurate and calibrated **when the data are generated by the kind of model
the forecasters assume or approximate**. It cannot show that real agents behave
like these simulators. A forecaster that fails here would be unfit for real data;
one that passes has only cleared a necessary bar.

## Design

- Four scenarios, parameters fixed before any forecaster output was inspected
  (`SCENARIOS` in
  [`forecast_interval_calibration.py`](https://github.com/MSKazemi/idkmesh/blob/main/experiments/forecast_interval_calibration.py)):
  `rasch_moderate`, `rasch_hard_tail` (wider difficulty spread, bigger blind spot),
  `rasch_unsolvable` (10% of tasks no agent can solve) and `family_clustered`
  (6 model families that fail together on a per-task shock).
- Each scenario draws 6 independent populations of 60 agents over 300 tasks. Truth
  is the exact union coverage of all 60 agents; the floor is its complement, so the
  floor error equals the coverage error.
- Per population and per k in {3, 5, 10, 20}: 30 random pilots. Each forecaster is
  scored by bias, mean absolute error (MAE), the share of pilots within 0.03, and
  the empirical coverage of its 95% percentile-bootstrap interval (100 replicates),
  resampling either tasks (`task`) or the pilot's agents (`agent`).
- Forecasters: `independence`, `beta_binomial`, `incidence` (Chao et al. 2014,
  Chao2-style) and `pilot_only` (observed coverage). The IRT/Rasch forecaster is
  excluded because it needs the full outcome matrix (see `forecast_intervals.py`).
- **Declared before the first full run:** tolerance 0.03; an interval is
  *calibrated* if pooled coverage is at least 0.90 against a nominal 0.95; the
  verdict at k = 10 is `falsified` if no forecaster/scheme has MAE <= 0.03 in any
  scenario, `supported` if some forecaster/scheme has MAE <= 0.03 **and** a
  calibrated interval in every scenario, `mixed` otherwise. No scenario or
  threshold was adjusted after the run.

## Result at k = 10: `mixed`

The recorded decision is **mixed**. The beta-binomial point forecast meets the
0.03 tolerance in three of four scenarios, but **no forecaster/scheme is both
within the tolerance and calibrated in any scenario at k = 10**. Exactly one
(scenario, forecaster/scheme) cell reaches the 0.90 coverage bar, and its point
forecast misses the tolerance.

| scenario | forecaster/scheme | bias | MAE | within 0.03 | 95% interval coverage | mean width |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rasch_moderate | `beta_binomial/task` | -0.0143 | 0.0163 | 0.84 | 0.478 | 0.033 |
| rasch_moderate | `beta_binomial/agent` | -0.0143 | 0.0163 | 0.84 | 0.367 | 0.101 |
| rasch_moderate | `incidence/task` | -0.0488 | 0.0514 | 0.34 | 0.628 | 0.102 |
| rasch_moderate | `incidence/agent` | -0.0488 | 0.0514 | 0.34 | 0.506 | 0.229 |
| rasch_hard_tail | `beta_binomial/task` | -0.0144 | 0.0283 | 0.59 | 0.761 | 0.082 |
| rasch_hard_tail | `beta_binomial/agent` | -0.0144 | 0.0283 | 0.59 | 0.483 | 0.128 |
| rasch_hard_tail | `incidence/task` | -0.0622 | 0.0700 | 0.21 | 0.678 | 0.163 |
| rasch_hard_tail | `incidence/agent` | -0.0622 | 0.0700 | 0.21 | 0.256 | 0.229 |
| rasch_unsolvable | `beta_binomial/task` | +0.0474 | 0.0482 | 0.18 | 0.267 | 0.058 |
| rasch_unsolvable | `beta_binomial/agent` | +0.0474 | 0.0482 | 0.18 | 0.939 | 0.117 |
| rasch_unsolvable | `incidence/task` | -0.0461 | 0.0504 | 0.30 | 0.722 | 0.135 |
| rasch_unsolvable | `incidence/agent` | -0.0461 | 0.0504 | 0.30 | 0.478 | 0.220 |
| family_clustered | `beta_binomial/task` | -0.0004 | 0.0086 | 0.97 | 0.672 | 0.020 |
| family_clustered | `beta_binomial/agent` | -0.0004 | 0.0086 | 0.97 | 0.550 | 0.068 |
| family_clustered | `incidence/task` | -0.0333 | 0.0367 | 0.48 | 0.817 | 0.087 |
| family_clustered | `incidence/agent` | -0.0333 | 0.0367 | 0.48 | 0.517 | 0.192 |

Reading the table (k = 10, pooled over 6 populations x 30 pilots):

- **Point accuracy.** The beta-binomial MAE is below 0.03 in `rasch_moderate`,
  `rasch_hard_tail` (0.0283, close to the line) and `family_clustered`. It fails in
  `rasch_unsolvable` (0.0482, bias +0.0474). A likely, untested explanation: tasks
  no agent can solve look to the method-of-moments fit like very hard tasks that
  more agents would eventually reach, so it over-forecasts. The Chao2-style
  `incidence` forecaster under-forecasts in every scenario (bias -0.033 to -0.062)
  and exceeds 0.03 in all four at k = 10.
- **Interval calibration fails.** Among the forecasters in the table, the `task` bootstrap, which models only
  task-sampling noise, covers 0.267 to 0.817 for these forecasters, far below
  0.95. The `agent` bootstrap is wider but does not fix it (0.256 to 0.939). The
  one high figure, 0.939 for `beta_binomial/agent` in `rasch_unsolvable`, belongs to
  a forecast whose bias is +0.0474, so the interval is wide enough to reach a
  truth the point estimate misses; it is not evidence of calibration.
- **Two failure patterns, not separated by this study.** Where the bias is large
  (`rasch_unsolvable`, `incidence` everywhere) a percentile interval around a biased
  estimator cannot cover the truth. Where the bias is negligible
  (`family_clustered` beta-binomial, bias -0.0004) coverage is still only 0.550 to
  0.672, so those bootstrap intervals are also too narrow for the pilot-to-pilot
  variation. Both need a different interval construction, not a wider nominal level.
- **`independence` looks accurate only where truth is near saturation** (it
  forecasts 1.0 and C(M) is 0.98 in two scenarios). It fails where the floor is
  about 12% (MAE 0.12), and its interval has zero width, so coverage is 0. Treat
  its "accurate in two scenarios" entry in the report as an artefact.
- **`pilot_only`** (the pilot's own coverage) has MAE 0.089 to 0.147 at k = 10, a
  similar magnitude to the 0.0965 gap E045 measured on real data.

## Exploratory, outside the declared rule: k = 20

H2 is stated for k <= 10, so k = 20 does not enter the verdict. For orientation,
the Chao2-style task bootstrap at k = 20:

| scenario | forecaster/scheme | bias | MAE | within 0.03 | 95% interval coverage | mean width |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rasch_moderate | `incidence/task` | -0.0116 | 0.0179 | 0.83 | 0.945 | 0.065 |
| rasch_hard_tail | `incidence/task` | -0.0199 | 0.0296 | 0.59 | 0.917 | 0.126 |
| rasch_unsolvable | `incidence/task` | -0.0116 | 0.0201 | 0.74 | 0.939 | 0.106 |
| family_clustered | `incidence/task` | -0.0091 | 0.0155 | 0.89 | 0.917 | 0.054 |

At k = 20 this forecaster is below 0.03 in all four scenarios and its interval
coverage is 0.917 to 0.945, so in simulation it is both accurate and calibrated
once the pilot is twice H2's size. That observation was made after the declared
rule was fixed and is a hypothesis for real data, not a finding.

## Controls

[`tests/test_x4_forecast_interval_calibration.py`](https://github.com/MSKazemi/idkmesh/blob/main/tests/test_x4_forecast_interval_calibration.py)
checks that the scoring cannot be fooled:

- an oracle that returns the truth scores MAE 0 and coverage 1;
- a constant-guess estimator and a sound estimator shifted by -0.2 are both
  caught on MAE, bias and tolerance rate, and the constant one on coverage;
- the verdict rule is exercised on hand-built inputs for `falsified`, `supported`
  and both `mixed` routes, and a broken estimator cannot win it end to end;
- the committed report's pooled rows and decision are recomputed from its recorded
  per-population cells, its constants and scenario parameters match the module,
  its analysis digests match the current source files, and the first two pilots of
  an audit cell are regenerated from the seed;
- every row of the two tables above is checked against the committed report.

## Limits

- **Synthetic.** Rasch-like generators with exact truth favour forecasters built on
  a per-item difficulty distribution. Real submissions are not exchangeable agents
  and share scaffolds and base models (E047).
- **Finite populations.** Truth is the 60-agent pool, as in E046. The latent
  large-population floor is not scored.
- **Pilots in a population share a truth**, so pooled coverage is not an average
  of independent trials. The report keeps per-population coverage and the worst
  population's coverage (`min_population_coverage`).
- **One coverage threshold** (0.90) and **one level** (0.95) were declared. Other
  interval constructions (for example bias-corrected or analytic iNEXT intervals)
  were not tried and may calibrate better; trying them now and keeping the best
  would be tuning on this run.
- The real-data question stays open until E048 provides fresh submissions. Rasch
  intervals remain unsupported.

## Reproduce

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python experiments/forecast_interval_calibration.py \
  --out experiments/results/X4-synthetic-interval-calibration.json
```

About ten minutes of single-core CPU; standard library only. The output is
[`experiments/results/X4-synthetic-interval-calibration.json`](https://github.com/MSKazemi/idkmesh/blob/main/experiments/results/X4-synthetic-interval-calibration.json).
